# Oia (Greece)

## Overview

Oia (Greek: Οία, sometimes spelled Ia, pronounced EE-ah) is what most people picture in their head when they think of Santorini, and even the Greek islands in general: steep volcanic cliffs with picturesque white houses, churches with deep blue roofs, and iconic windmills that appear lost in time. It is the northernmost town on Thira, the main island in the Santorini archipelago, about 200 km south of the Greek mainland. Oia's narrow streets scattered with arts and gift shops draw over 2 million tourists every year.

## Understand

### History

The recorded history of Oia dates back to the 13th century, when the Duchy of Naxos was established by the Venetians and feudal rule was instituted on Santorini. Apanomeria, also known as Agios Nikolaos castle, was one of the 5 local citadels and the ruins of its residential keep is the oldest part of modern Oia. The Ottoman Empire took over control in 1579, and the name Apanomeria found general acceptance until it was changed to the present-day Oia in the mid 19th century.

Peace brought prosperity at the end of the 19th century. Oia's harbour, Amoudi Bay, developed as an important centre for maritime trade, a position boosted by the declining importance of Venice as a Mediterranean trading hub. It was part of trading routes from Alexandria to as far as Russia. By the turn of the 19th century, the fleet consisted of 130 sailing ships and a wharf was set up in Amoudi Bay. Agriculture on Santorini produced wines of excellent quality which were exported to mainland Europe.

The economic boom came to an end with the arrival of steam power, which made sailing ships redundant. Oia lost trade to Piraeus and Laurium, and the resulting exodus led to a decline in population which in turn hurt the agricultural sector. A shift to fishing brought relief, but by the early 20th century this also started to decline due to overfishing. The World Wars brought economic depression and further emigration to the Greek mainland.

The earthquake of 9 July 1956, with a magnitude of 7.8, dealt the finishing blow to Oia. The strongest aftershock had a magnitude of 7.2, with an epicentre just off the coast north of Oia. Nearly the entire town was levelled, and population fell to an all time low of only 306 inhabitants by 1977. It remained largely abandoned for 2 decades.

### Redevelopment

A bold decision was made to rebuild the village from the ground up as a tourist attraction. The surviving churches and castle ruins were restored, and infrastructure was developed. Utilities such as electricity and telecommunications cables were laid underground, making the village "picture perfect".

Present-day Oia still does not have many residents. It is largely an artificial town, consisting almost entirely of tourist accommodation, restaurants, bars, and shops. It attracts huge crowds of tourists looking for the "perfect" photograph, and is immensely popular as a honeymoon destination, particularly with wealthy Indian and Chinese couples who flock here to take wedding pictures. Unsurprisingly, Oia's narrow streets are lined with jewellery shops, exclusive boutiques, and overpriced souvenirs made in China.

## Get in

KTEL Santorini, the public bus service, runs a route from Fira to **Oia Bus Station**, every hour or so. The fare is €2 per person, uncharacteristically cheap for Santorini. It is, however, cash-only, so have some coins for the correct fare.

A taxi from the port costs around €20 per group; €25 if you don't want driver to take people from other groups into the car. A taxi from the airport will set you back €30-40.

The road between Fira and Oia is meandering -- keep in mind if you choose between taxi and a rented car when you arrive exhausted after a ferry.

The most rewarding way into town in to walk the footpath along the caldera from Fira. It will take awhile as there some stairs to climb (especially in the way back), but the view is worth it.

## Get around

Motorised vehicles are banned from the town, and the only way to get around is by foot. Even garbage is taken away by donkeys. Donkeys can be rented on the staircase connecting Armeni bay to Oia. However, walking gives the most freedom to look around and enjoy the scenery.

During the touristic season, which starts in April and ends in October, the town often becomes so crowded that congestion occurs in the narrow streets. Don't even try to push a bicycle through during the summer months! Fortunately, Oia is rather small so traversing the town only takes 20-30 minutes by foot.

## See

Oia is a picturesque little town. Spend at least a few hours exploring by meandering up and down paths and staircases. Along the way you'll see interesting architecture and breathtaking views of the caldera.

- **Amoudi Bay** (Órmos Ammoúdi, Όρμος Αμμούδι) — The most picturesque bay on Thira, the moon shaped bay is surrounded by barren cliffs and a lovely promenade with restaurants. Fantastic spot to watch the famous sunset, although it tends to get crowded for that reason in the evenings. (price: Free)
- **Londsa Castle** (Kasteli Agios Nikolaos) — Ruins of a Byzantine fortress that are a prominent landmark in the oldest part of town. It offers fantastic sunset views in the evening. The history of the castle dates back to the 15th century, and it survived for almost 600 years. Unfortunately, like many other structures on Santorini, it was largely levelled by the 1956 earthquake. Only the base of a tower and facade remain of what was once an impressive defensive structure built to protect the island from pirate attacks. (price: Free; hours: 24/7)
- **Armeni Bay** — Harder to reach than Amoudi Bay, and therefore far less crowded. Limited facilities, but great for a swim. (price: Free)
- **Katharos Beach** — Quiet pebble stone beach with the adjacent Katharos Lounge which serves great food. (price: Free)
- **Agios Georgios Church** — Christian Orthodox church. (price: Free)
- **Naval Museum** — Archaeology museum in a converted traditional house in the centre of town. Its collection features figureheads, seamen's chests, historic maritime equipment, drawings and maps, and models of old and new Thirean ships. There is special attention to the history of the Hellenic Navy. (hours: Wed-Mon 10:00-14:00, 17:00-20:00)
- **Panagia Platsani** (Ιερός Ναός Παναγίας του Ακάθιστου Ύμνου) — Greek Orthodox church. (price: Free)
- **Saint Georgios Oia Holy Orthodox Church** (Ιερός Ναός Αγίου Γεωργίου Οίας) — Greek Orthodox church. (price: Free)

## Do

- **Fira - Oia Hike** — By far the most spectacular hike on Thira is the 11 km trail along the crater rim to Fira. It offers some breathtaking views over the caldera, and the other islands of the archipelago. At a moderate walking pace, the hike takes about 3 hours. The trail starts at Amoudi Bay and follows the rim east through town. It is well-marked; you'll see a few "To Fira" signs as you leave town. Going from Fira to Oia is more uphill, with a climb of over 300 m, so this direction is considered a bit easier. (price: Free)

## Drink

Likes everywhere else on Santorini, the tap water is of marginal quality in Oia, despite its new €813,000 EU-sponsored **desalination plant**. Bottles of water can be purchased in every mini market, and most hotels offer complimentary bottles of water to their guests.

### Wine

A number of vineyards on Santorini produce great wines. Sample the local wines when possible!

- **Fino** — Cocktail bar & restaurant. (hours: 17:00 - 01:30 daily)

## Sleep

The village has a variety of accommodation options: Cave style houses updated with modern features, cliff side houses that form a small hotel, mansions once owned by sea captains converted to luxury suites. The main point to consider is if the accommodation faces the sea, caldera and volcano. You may also want a private or shared terrace for breakfast or lounging, big windows (some of the cave houses have small viewpoints), and a location that's not too far down the cliff if stairs don't suit you well.

The protected village architecture and the cliffs restrict the building of swimming pools, so there are very few offered on the caldera side. If you need it, there's a public pool-bar offering views towards the sunset side of the island.

## Stay safe

During the tourist season, the narrow streets are packed with tourists and body contact is not uncommon, which makes it easy for thieves to lift valuables out of the pockets of unsuspecting travellers. The street layout also makes for an ideal escape route, as pickpockets can easily vanish in the labyrinth of narrow passages and steep steps. Leave valuables home or wear them under your shirt in a neck pouch. Do not rely on hip pouches, they are an easy target in crowded areas. Never leave electronics such as cameras unattended.
