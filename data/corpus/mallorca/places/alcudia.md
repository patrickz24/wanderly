# Alcúdia

## Overview

Alcúdia is a resort in the Raiguer district of Mallorca, near the northeast tip of the island. With a population of 21,300 in 2023, it has a well-preserved walled old town, and a long hotel strip on its sandy beach.

## Understand

When the Roman Empire crumbled, their town of Pollentia was repeatedly attacked, until it was abandoned for a better-defended site 10 km inland: this is the present town of Pollença. The only habitation near the ruins was a Moorish farmstead on the hill above, al-kudja in Arabic. In 1229 Jaume I of Aragon muscled in on the territory, and from 1298 Jaume II built the walled town of Alcúdia.

Not much happened for the next 700 years, so it remained a small town and port unspoilt by industry. A few tourists arrived from the 1920s but the big boom, as elsewhere on Mallorca, was from the 1970s. A rash of hotels spread along the beach but didn't invade the old town, while the port acquired a leisure marina. There are bars a-plenty but Alcúdia never wanted the frenetic clubbing scene of Magaluf. It's a relaxing place popular with young families and with pensioners.

## Get in

Alcúdia is 55 km northeast of Palma via Ma-13, a good highway.

TIB Bus 302 runs from Palma de Mallorca every 20-30 min, taking an hour via Inca and Sa Pobla. It continues to the port, beach strip and Can Picafort, but you may not ride it just for that section.

Bus 322 runs from Pollença every 25 min to Port de Pollença, Alcúdia town centre, port and beach. Bus 324 runs from Can Picafort every 15 min, and Bus 325 hourly from Cala Rajada via Can Picafort.

Bus 315 runs every 50 min from Sa Pobla, taking an hour via Can Picafort, the beach and port to Alcúdia town centre.

Bus 231 runs from Sóller March-Oct four times a day, taking two hours via Mirador de ses Barques, Escorca, Lluc, and the town and port of Pollença.

**Port de Alcúdia** has daily Balearia car ferries from Barcelona. The quickest takes only 3 hr 30 min and is the most expensive; other sailings take 6-7 hours and may be on a triangular route via Menorca.

Menorca Lines sail daily in the morning from Alcúdia to Ciutadella on Menorca, taking 75 min. They return in the evening so a day-trip is possible in that direction.

## Get around

Local buses include 322 from Pollença, 324 from Can Picafort and 325 from Cala Rajada, as above. Bus 323 also shuttles every 50 min between Alcúdia, the port, and Alcanada east on the headland. You may not use Bus 302 from Palma for local short hops.

Over a dozen places offer car hire or bicycle hire. The narrow old town streets are pedestrianised.

Taxi firms here are mostly in the business of airport transfers not short rides.

## See

- City walls encircle the old town, with a free walkway Camí de Ronda open 24 hours. Built 1298 to 1362, they're best preserved to the north and west, where two of the four city gates also survive. An outer wall was added in the 16th century but later demolished.
- **Church of Sant Jaume** — Church founded in 1302 and incorporated into the city walls, so it doubled as a watchtower. It was undermined by floods in 1870 and collapsed, and re-built in 1890s neo-gothic. The museum displays religious art.
- **Bull ring** (Plaça de Toros) — This was built in the 1920s but no longer has bullfights. It's occasionally open for cultural events.
- **Porta del Moll** is one of the surviving city gates, two towers about an entrance through the walls.
- **Roman Pollentia** — A Roman camp here was rebuilt as a civilian town from 70 BC, when this was Mallorca's main port for trade with Rome and the east Med, while Palma was the port for North Africa and Spain. There's a residential area, necropolis and small amphitheatre. Enter from the north through the town walls. (price: Adult €4, conc or child €2.50; hours: May-Oct Mon-Fri 08:30-19:30, Sat Sun 08:30-14:00)
- Museu Monogràfic next to the Church of Sant Jaume displays findings from Roman Pollentia.
- **Municipal Cemetery** is a riot of florid funerary monuments. It's open daily 08:00-18:00.
- **Alcanada Lighthouse** was completed in 1861 and automated in 1960. It's on an islet 150 m out, within swimming or SUP distance.
- **Museu sa Bassa Blanca** (Yannick and Ben Jakober Foundation) — Art museum with a notable "Nins" gallery (portraits of children by Old Masters), Socrates gallery exploring relativistic space-time, and modern sculpture park. (price: Adult €7, conc or child €5; hours: Wed-Sat 10:00-18:00, Sun 10:00-15:00)
- **Ermita de la Victoria** was built as a watchtower in 1679 and is now a baroque church. You can drive here or take various hiking or cycling trails onto the forested headland. There's a restaurant adjacent.
- **Talaia d'Alcúdia** is the peak on the headland. The simplest ascent is from L'Ermita and might take 30 minutes.
- **End Point** is the evocative name of the tip of Alcanada peninsula. Just north, tiny Zero Island is probably so named to prevent visitors wasting their time reaching it.
- **Gran Irland** strange to say is not a Dublin-themed pub, but an islet in the lagoon reached by a causeway from the west. Wear scruffy old trainers, as the water laps over the causeway. Visitors often find it smelly and trash-strewn.
- **Parc Natural de s'Albufera** — Wetland wildlife reserve, with bird hides and hiking trails. Some trails are lengthy so it's worth bringing a bike. (price: Free; hours: Daily 09:00-17:00)

## Do

- Alcúdia beach is a sandy bay 14 km long stretching to C'an Picafort, with Playa de Muro midway. The shore elsewhere is rocky.
- Golf: Alcanada GC is a parkland course on the road to the lighthouse. White tees 6559 m, par 72.
- Football: UD Alcúdia play soccer in Tercera División – Group 11, the fifth tier. They play at Els Arcs, capacity 1750. For a top tier game head into Palma.
- Boat trips: a dozen agencies around the marina offer dolphin-watching, sunset cruises and the like.

### Events

- Fira de la Sípia is a seafood festival in mid-April.
- Ironman Triathlon is held between Alcúdia and Palma in May.
- Festival of St Jaume is over nine days in July. The apostle James the Great was martyred in 44 AD, with his pattern day taken as 25 July in western Christendom. He's the patron saint of Spain, where he's called Santiago, from Sancti Iacobi the church or sanctuary of Saint James. The town is decorated and there are various concerts, theatre, exhibitions, religious ceremonies and fireworks.
- Triennial of Holy Christ is a religious procession where the population parades barefoot through town in silence. In 1507, goes the legend, an image of Christ sweated blood and water to relieve a drought, as apparently a simple downpour was beyond his divine powers. The next is in July 2028 then 2031 and so on.
- Alcúdia Jazz Festival is held in September.
- Agricultural fair is in early October

## Buy

- Lidl is the main supermarket, at the roundabout 500 m from the beach, open daily 09:00-22:00.
- Alcúdia Market is held next to Porta del Moll Su & Tu 08:30-13:30.

## Eat

About 20 restaurants in the old town centre, a similar number in Port d'Alcúdia around the marina, and a dozen or so further south along Playa de Muro. Many close down out of season.

## Drink

No free-standing pubs in the old town centre, but a dozen at the port and more down the beach strip.
