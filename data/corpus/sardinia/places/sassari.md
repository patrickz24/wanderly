# Sassari

## Overview

Sassari is a city in Sardinia. It is one of the oldest cities in Sardinia.

## Understand

Sassari is a city in the northwest corner of Sardinia and a capital of the homonymous province. It is the second-largest city in Sardinia in terms of population with over 130,000 inhabitants.

### Tourist information

- **Infosassari** (Ufficio Informazioni Turistiche) (hours: Tue-Fri 09:00-13:30, 15:00-18:00; Sat 09:00-13:30)

## Get in

### By plane

Alghero Airport (AHO), about 33 km away is the nearest to Sassari. Another relatively close airport is located in Olbia (105 km).

### By boat

From ferry terminal in Porto Torres.

### By train

There are plenty of regional trains, operated by Trenitalia, that connects with Olbia in the northwestern part of the Sardinia and with Cagliari in the south. Also a local narrow gauge line between Alghero (35 min) and Sorso runs via this station. This line is operated by ARST.

- **Sassari railway station** (Stazione di Sassari)

### By bus

- **Stazione dei pullman** (regional bus hub) — Simply a series of bus stop with a ticket booth.

## Get around

### By bus

- **ATP Sassari** — Provides urban bus services for the city. (price: 90 min single €1.30 (onboard €1.80); 2 hr (incl. Metrosassari) €2.00; daily pass (incl. Metrosassari) €3.30)

### By tram

- **Metrosassari** — The city has a short tram line. It's not very useful though for tourists at it reaches the city centre after quite a bit of diversion into a periphery. (price: 90 min ticket €1.30; hours: 06:50-21:00)

### By car

The city center has a limited traffic zone (ZTL) (map and hours). Motorcycles are allowed, but most other vehicles are prohibited from accessing it most of the time.

## See

### Old town

- **Cattedrale di San Nicola** (The Cathedral of St. Nicholas of Bari) — The cathedral was built in the 13th century and enlarged in Catalan Gothic style from 1480; there's a monument to the Duca di Moriana inside. The façade, belonging to the Baroque Spanish colonial restorations of 1650–1723, has a rectangular portico surmounted by three niches housing statues of saints. The bell tower is in Romanesque style.
- **Fontana di Rosello** (Font of Rosello) — The 17th-century fountain built at the ancient source. The fountain is considered the symbol of the city. At the top of two crossing arches there is a statue of St. Gavino.
- **Palazzo Ducale** — Now the City Hall. (hours: Mon-Fri 08:00-19:30; Sat 08:00-13:00)

**Le Stanze e le Cantine del Duca** (Duke's rooms and cellars) — A part of the Civic Museum. (price: €3; hours: Tue-Sat 10:00-13:00 15:00-18:00; Sun 10:00-13:00 (guided tours every 30 minutes))

- **Palazzo di Città** (Museo della Città) — Entering from Corso Vittorio Emanuele II visitors could see the interiors of the Palazzo di Città including the ball room, the historic Civic Theatre. Another entrance is from Via Sebastiano Satta through the tourist information office. This part presents some Sardinian costumes and two works of Eugenio Tavolara depicting the Carnival and the Holy Week in Sassari. (price: €3; hours: Tue 10:00-13:00; Wed-Fri 10:00-13:00 15:00-18:00; Sat 10:00-13:00)

**Teatro Civico**

- **Palazzo Tola** — There are still some traces there of its initial Renaissance façade.
- **Chiesa di Santa Maria di Bethlem** — Despite its modest exterior it's richly decorated inside.
- **Vecchie mura** — A fragment of the Pisan city walls.

### Other sites

- **Museo Nazionale G.A. Sanna** — Largest museum institution in the north-central Sardinia in size and importance of its scientific collections, and an important cultural reference for the North of the island. Closed for restoration work as of late February, 2020 with no reopening date. (price: €3; hours: Tue-Sat 09:00-20:00, 1st Sun 09:00-20:00)
- **Pinacoteca Mus'a al Canopoleno** (Museo Sassari Arte) (price: €2.50; hours: Mon-Tue 09:00-13:30 15:00-17:00; Wed-Fri 09:00-13:30)
- **Palazzo dell'Università**
- **Palazzo della Provincia** — Built in 1872.
- **Casa Cugurra** (Palazzo Cugurra) — Built in 1890 in Neo-Baroque style with 1906 extension in Liberty style.
- **Villa Sant’Elia** (villa Mimosa) — A villa in Liberty style.
- **Padiglione per l'artigianato "Eugenio Tavolara"** (Padiglione dell'Artigianato Isola) — Tavolara pavilion for handicrafts and design is the headquarters and the exhibition space of I.S.O.L.A. (Sardinian craftsmanship institution). Built in 1956 by the project of Italian architect Ubaldo Badas.
